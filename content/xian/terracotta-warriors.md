## Overview

The Terracotta Army is the single most important reason to stop in Xi'an. Buried for over 2,200 years, the thousands of life-sized clay soldiers were built to guard Emperor Qin Shi Huang in the afterlife — the same emperor who unified China and began the Great Wall. Discovered by farmers digging a well in 1974, the site is now a UNESCO World Heritage complex about 40km east of the city centre.

There are three excavation pits. Pit 1 is the main event: a football-field-sized hangar containing roughly 6,000 warriors arranged in battle formation. Every figure has a unique face, hairstyle, and posture. Pit 2 holds cavalry, archers, and chariots in various stages of excavation. Pit 3 is the smallest — the command headquarters. A separate exhibition hall displays two half-scale bronze chariots, among the finest bronze castings ever found.

The scale is what gets you. Photographs do not prepare you for standing at the edge of Pit 1 and looking across rows of soldiers stretching into the distance. It is one of those places where the physical experience is genuinely different from seeing pictures.

## What to See & Do

- **Start with Pit 1** — the largest and most dramatic. Walk around the full perimeter for different angles.
- **Pit 2 and Pit 3** — smaller but worth seeing. Pit 2 has better-preserved individual figures, including the famous kneeling archer.
- **Bronze Chariots Exhibition Hall** — don't skip this. The detail on the half-scale chariots is extraordinary.
- **Hire a guide or use the audio guide** — the history adds a lot. English-speaking guides are available at the entrance (¥150–200 for the group).
- **Skip the "factory" shops** — tour buses stop at jade or terracotta reproduction factories. They are overpriced tourist traps.

## Best Time to Visit

Early morning, before the tour bus wave arrives around 10–11am. The museum opens at 8:30am (March–November). In July, it will be hot and crowded by midday. Getting there by 9am gives you the best experience.

## Practical Tips

- **Tickets:** ¥120/person (March–November). Book online in advance via the official WeChat mini-program or at the ticket office.
- **Duration:** 2–3 hours for a thorough visit.
- **Getting there:** ~40km east of Xi'an city centre, about 1 hour by car. The museum has large parking lots.
- **Bags:** Small bags are fine. Large luggage can be stored at your hotel.
- **Photography:** Allowed everywhere. No flash in the pits.
- **Food:** There are restaurants inside the complex, but nothing remarkable. Eat before or after.

## Why It's Worth It

This is a once-in-a-lifetime sight. No reproduction, documentary, or photograph conveys what it feels like to stand in front of thousands of individually crafted soldiers that have been underground since 210 BC. For a group with only one day in Xi'an, this is the non-negotiable stop.
