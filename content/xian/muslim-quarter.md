## Overview

The Muslim Quarter is Xi'an's most famous food street and one of the liveliest neighbourhoods in the city. Located just northwest of the Drum Tower, it is the historic centre of Xi'an's Hui Muslim community — descendants of Silk Road traders who settled here over a thousand years ago. The result is a cuisine that blends Central Asian and Chinese traditions: lamb, flatbreads, hand-pulled noodles, cumin-spiced skewers, and pomegranate juice.

The main street (北院门, Beiyuanmen) is a dense, narrow lane packed with stalls, smoke, noise, and crowds. Vendors pull noodles by hand in shop windows, grill lamb skewers over charcoal, and hammer walnut candy on stone slabs. It is not a quiet dining experience — it is a street food crawl where you eat standing up, move between stalls, and try things that look good.

Behind the main tourist street, the side alleys are calmer and often have better food. The Great Mosque (大清真寺), one of the oldest and largest mosques in China, is tucked inside these alleys — a beautiful blend of Chinese garden architecture and Islamic design.

## What to See & Do

- **Eat your way through the main street** — this is a grazing experience, not a sit-down meal. Try small portions from many stalls.
- **Must-try foods:**
  - **羊肉泡馍 (yángròu pàomó)** — crumbled flatbread soaked in lamb broth, Xi'an's signature dish
  - **肉夹馍 (ròujiāmó)** — "Chinese hamburger", minced meat in a crispy flatbread
  - **凉皮 (liángpí)** — cold rice noodles with chilli oil and vinegar
  - **烤羊肉串 (kǎo yángròu chuàn)** — cumin lamb skewers grilled over charcoal
  - **石榴汁 (shíliú zhī)** — fresh pomegranate juice, pressed on the spot
  - **镜糕 (jìnggāo)** — tiny steamed rice cakes with various toppings
- **Visit the Great Mosque (大清真寺)** — ¥25 entry. A tranquil Chinese-Islamic garden complex, a world away from the chaos outside.
- **Explore the side alleys** — less crowded, more local, often better food than the main drag.

## Best Time to Visit

Evening is the best time. The street comes alive after 5pm as the heat drops and the night market energy builds. By 7pm it is at full intensity — packed, smoky, loud, and exactly what you came for. It stays busy until 10–11pm.

## Practical Tips

- **Entry:** Free (the street is public). Great Mosque is ¥25.
- **Duration:** 2–3 hours for eating, browsing, and the mosque.
- **Payment:** WeChat Pay / Alipay at most stalls. Some smaller vendors prefer cash.
- **Crowds:** It will be packed in the evening, especially in summer. Stay together as a group.
- **Halal:** All food on the main street is halal. No pork anywhere in the quarter.
- **Location:** Immediately behind the Drum Tower, in the centre of old Xi'an. Walking distance from the City Wall South Gate (~15min).

## Why It's Worth It

The Muslim Quarter is where Xi'an's Silk Road heritage becomes tangible. For a group of Italians who care about food, this is the most directly engaging experience in the city — you can taste the fusion of cultures that made Xi'an one of the ancient world's great crossroads. It is also the perfect final evening meal before heading west on the Silk Road yourselves.
