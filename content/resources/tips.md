# Travel Tips · First Time in China

Practical advice for the 5 Italian group members visiting China for the first time. Your Chinese friends will handle most logistics, but knowing these things makes everything smoother.

---

## Before You Go

| Topic | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **VPN** | Install and test a VPN before entering China. Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, and most Western social media are blocked. Download it at home — you cannot install one inside China. |
| **WeChat** | Download WeChat (微信) and set it up before the trip. It's used for messaging, payments, restaurant menus, booking tickets, and calling taxis. Your Chinese friends can help you link a payment method. |
| **Alipay** | International visitors can now link foreign credit cards to Alipay. Set this up before departure — it's accepted everywhere. |
| **Offline maps** | Download Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps. Google Maps works poorly in China even with a VPN — location data is offset. |
| **12306 app** | The official train booking app. Your Chinese friends will likely handle bookings, but having it installed helps. |
| **Travel insurance** | Get comprehensive coverage including medical evacuation. Hospital visits require upfront payment in China. |

## Money & Payments

- **Cash is king in rural areas.** Withdraw ¥2,000–3,000 per person in Beijing or Xi'an. ATMs are scarce on the northwest loop.
- **WeChat Pay / Alipay** is accepted almost everywhere in cities — even tiny street food stalls.
- **Credit cards** (Visa/Mastercard) are rarely accepted at shops or restaurants. Only useful at ATMs and some hotels.
- **Tipping is not expected** anywhere in China. Don't tip at restaurants, hotels, or taxis.
- **Bargaining** is expected at markets like Panjiayuan (Beijing) and parts of the Muslim Quarter (Xi'an). Not appropriate at shops with price tags.

## Food & Drink

- **Tap water is not drinkable.** Always use bottled water, even for brushing teeth in rural areas.
- **Hot water is everywhere.** Hotels, trains, and restaurants all provide free hot/boiled water (开水, kāishuǐ). Carry a reusable bottle.
- **Spice levels** vary by region. Xi'an and the northwest are moderately spicy. Say 不辣 (bù là, "not spicy") or 微辣 (wēi là, "mild spicy") when ordering.
- **Breakfast is savory** in China. Expect noodles, dumplings, congee, and flatbread — not pastries and coffee. Embrace it.
- **Group dining** is the norm. Dishes are shared from the centre of the table. Order many dishes and share everything.
- **Chopsticks** are standard. Forks are available at tourist-friendly restaurants but not everywhere. Practice before the trip.

## Transport

- **Didi** (滴滴) is China's Uber. Works well in Beijing and Xi'an. The app has an English interface. Your Chinese friend can also call one via their WeChat.
- **Trains** are excellent. Book on 12306.cn. Arrive 30+ minutes early — stations have airport-style security screening. Bring your passport — you need it to enter.
- **Driving** — foreigners cannot drive in China. Only your Chinese friends can drive the rental car.
- **Traffic** is aggressive by European standards. Pedestrians do not have automatic right-of-way. Look both ways, always.

## Health & Safety

- **Altitude sickness** affects some people at Qinghai Lake and Chaka (3,200m). Symptoms: headache, shortness of breath, dizziness. Take it easy on Day 1 of the northwest loop. Drink water, avoid alcohol.
- **Heat exhaustion** is a real risk in Dunhuang (38–42°C) and Xi'an (38°C). Stay indoors noon–5pm in Dunhuang. Carry water everywhere.
- **Sunburn** happens fast at altitude and in the desert. SPF 50+, reapply every 2 hours.
- **Pharmacies** (药店, yàodiàn) are on every main street. Most common medicines are available without prescription.
- **Hospitals** require upfront payment. Carry your insurance details and enough cash/card for emergency treatment.
- **Emergency numbers:** 120 (ambulance), 110 (police), 119 (fire).

## Cultural Notes

- **Toilet etiquette** — carry tissue paper. Many public toilets outside cities are squat-style and don't provide paper. Some charge ¥1.
- **Queuing** is less strict than in Italy. People may cut in line — don't take it personally, just hold your ground politely.
- **Photography** — always ask before photographing people, especially monks, ethnic minorities, or children. No cameras inside Mogao Caves (strictly enforced).
- **Smoking** is common in China, especially among men. Non-smoking areas in restaurants are not always enforced.
- **"Lǎowài"** (老外) means "foreigner" — you'll hear it often. It's neutral, not rude.
- **Gift culture** — if someone offers you food, tea, or a small gift, accept it graciously. Refusing can be seen as impolite.

## Connectivity

- **Buy a local SIM** at Beijing Daxing Airport on arrival. China Mobile or China Unicom — ~¥100–200 for a tourist SIM with 10–20GB data. Much more reliable than relying on a VPN + foreign SIM.
- **WiFi** is available at all hotels and most restaurants. Quality varies in rural areas.
- **WhatsApp/Signal** won't work without a VPN. Use WeChat to message within the group.
- **Calling home** — use VPN + WhatsApp or FaceTime, or WeChat video call (works without VPN).

## Beijing-Specific Tips

- **Forbidden City** — book weeks ahead in July (40,000/day cap). No large bags allowed. Wear comfortable shoes — it's a lot of walking.
- **Great Wall (Mutianyu)** — arrive before 9am. Take the toboggan down. Bring water.
- **Panjiayuan Market** — assume everything is a reproduction. Buy what you like, not what you think is "real."
- **Peking duck** — book Da Dong or another top restaurant in advance for a group of 6+.

---

## Xi'an-Specific Tips

- **Terracotta Warriors** — book tickets online. Hire a guide at the entrance (¥150–200 for the group). Skip the "factory" shops.
- **City Wall cycling** — rent bikes at the South Gate. Bring water — there's almost no shade on the wall. Return bikes at any gate.
- **Muslim Quarter** — all food is halal (no pork). Best after 5pm. The side alleys have better food than the main tourist street.
- **大唐不夜城** — go after 8pm when it's fully dark. Charge your phone fully — you'll take hundreds of photos.
- **Xi'an North Station** is different from Xi'an Station. The D2685 to Xining departs from Xi'an North (北站). Allow time for the taxi between them.

---

## Northwest-Specific Tips

- **Fuel up** completely in Dachaidan before the Qaidam Basin crossing — no stations for ~300km.
- **Dunhuang heat** — do not be outside noon–5pm. All sightseeing before 11am or after 5pm.
- **Mogao Caves** — no cameras, no phones, no bags inside the caves. Strictly enforced.
- **Chaka Salt Lake** — go at sunrise for the mirror reflection. Wear old shoes, salt brine ruins everything.
- **Altitude** — Qinghai Lake and Chaka are at 3,200m. Take it easy, drink water, avoid running.
- **Qilian morning** — bring a warm layer. 2,800m at 8am in July is ~10°C.
